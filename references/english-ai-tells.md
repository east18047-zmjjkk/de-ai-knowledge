# English AI Writing Tells — Knowledge Reference

Common patterns that make English text sound machine-generated. Use as a diagnostic checklist when reviewing or rewriting.

**Evidence note**: Most items below are Level A or B (high consensus or common observation across public humanizer lists and model-output audits 2024–2026). See `evidence-basis.md` for grading and sources. Apply only when clustered; single natural uses are fine.

## High-Priority Lexical Tells

| Pattern | Evidence | Why it signals AI | Human alternative |
|---------|----------|-------------------|-------------------|
| delve / delve into | A | Extremely rare in natural speech at AI frequency | look at, examine, explore, dig into |
| tapestry / rich tapestry | A | Classic AI metaphor | Delete or use concrete description |
| leverage (as verb) | A | Corporate filler | use, apply, build on |
| robust | B | Empty quality claim | strong, solid, reliable, or specific |
| seamless / seamlessly | A | Marketing word | smooth, easy, without friction |
| underscore | A | Academic overreach | show, highlight, emphasize |
| pivotal / crucial / vital | B | Overused emphasis | key, important, or just state the point |
| realm | A | Rare in speech | area, field, world |
| embark on a journey | A | Journey metaphor | start, begin |
| harness / foster / unlock / unleash | A | Vague aspirational verbs | specific action verb |
| transformative / game-changer | A | Empty hype | describe the actual change |
| moreover / furthermore / additionally | A | Stiff sentence openers | also, and, plus, or restructure |
| it is important to note that / it's worth noting that | A | Throat-clearing | Delete and state the point |
| in today's fast-paced / digital / ever-changing world | A | Tired opener | Delete or be specific |
| at its core / at the heart of | A | Faux-depth | Restructure |
| not just X, it's Y / it's not X, it's Y | A | Most common structural tell | State the actual point directly |
| a myriad of / a wide range of | B | Padding | many, several, or list them |
| in conclusion / to summarize | B | Textbook signpost | End naturally |

## Structural Patterns

- Uniform sentence length (low burstiness)
- Forced triads ("fast, simple, powerful")
- Excessive em-dash trailing clauses
- Hedging openers ("It can be argued that", "Some might say")
- Faux-insight setups ("Here's the thing", "What nobody tells you")
- Paragraph openers that announce the topic instead of stating it
- Present participial trailing clauses (", making it...", ", highlighting...")
- Binary contrasts / negative parallelism overuse

## Positive Human Signals

- Contractions where natural
- Specific, concrete nouns and details
- Sentence length variation (short + longer)
- Mild opinions or qualifications when appropriate
- Spoken-like transitions
- Occasional imperfect phrasing

## Genre Notes

**Academic**: Precision is fine; dense hedging, formulaic transitions, and empty significance inflation are not.

**Narrative**: Prefer showing over telling. Avoid stock body beats and over-explained emotions.

Apply only when patterns appear in clusters. Single natural uses are acceptable.
