# Detection Checklist — English AI Flavor

Use this as a quick pass over a draft. Mark only when the pattern is clustered or formulaic. Single natural uses are not automatic flags.

## 1. Lexical Scan (Level A priority)

- [ ] delve / delve into
- [ ] tapestry / rich tapestry
- [ ] leverage (verb), unlock, unleash, harness, foster
- [ ] robust, seamless, transformative, game-changer, cutting-edge
- [ ] underscore, pivotal, crucial (as empty emphasis)
- [ ] realm, embark on a journey
- [ ] moreover / furthermore / additionally as sentence openers
- [ ] it is important to note that / it's worth noting that
- [ ] in today's fast-paced / digital / ever-changing world
- [ ] at its core / at the heart of
- [ ] not just X, it's Y / it's not X, it's Y (and close variants)
- [ ] a myriad of / a wide range of
- [ ] in conclusion / to summarize (as mechanical closer)

## 2. Structural Scan

- [ ] Three or more consecutive sentences of nearly identical length
- [ ] Forced triads or rule-of-three lists that feel padded
- [ ] Frequent trailing present-participial clauses (", making...", ", highlighting...")
- [ ] Multiple hedging openers ("It can be argued", "Some might say", "While there are certainly")
- [ ] Faux-insight or staged run-up ("Here's the thing", "What nobody tells you")
- [ ] Paragraphs that open by announcing the topic instead of stating a claim
- [ ] Repeated binary contrast / negative parallelism across paragraphs
- [ ] Overuse of em-dashes for dramatic pauses or trailing clauses

## 3. Genre-Specific Checks

**Academic / papers**
- [ ] Dense formulaic hedging that adds no precision
- [ ] Empty significance inflation ("plays a pivotal role", "underscores the importance")
- [ ] Stock transitions that could be cut without loss

**Narrative / stories**
- [ ] Stock body or emotion beats ("her heart hammered", "a breath she didn't know she was holding")
- [ ] Over-explained feelings instead of concrete action or detail
- [ ] Uniform rhythm that flattens scene pacing

## 4. Positive Human Signals (presence is good)

- [ ] Natural contractions where register allows
- [ ] Concrete nouns, numbers, or specific details
- [ ] Visible sentence-length variation
- [ ] Occasional mild opinion, qualification, or aside that fits the voice
- [ ] Transitions that feel spoken or simply omitted rather than announced

## 5. Decision Rule

- Cluster of Level A items or multiple structural flags → revise.
- Isolated Level A or mostly Level B/C → note only; preserve if natural.
- After revision, re-scan once. Do not keep polishing until every possible tell is gone.

## Quick Scoring (optional)

- 0–1 flags: likely fine or light pass.
- 2–4 flags: targeted rewrite of marked spots.
- 5+ flags or heavy structural uniformity: fuller pass recommended, still preserving meaning and register.
